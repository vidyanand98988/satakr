DIVA decay model

How fast does treated straw break down?

Our hypothesis: cold plasma plus the Pusa Decomposer reaches field readiness (about 30% mass loss) around day 10, where the decomposer alone needs day 25. It is built from cuticle permeability mechanics, Chen et al. (2018, Scientific Reports) on DBD wax modification, and a two-pool decay fit to published rice straw litterbag data.

No published study couples cold plasma pretreatment with in-field microbial decomposition of paddy straw yet. Baseline is untreated field decay. The Year 1 pilot is how we find out if it holds. The 14-day simulator on the home page runs on this model.

Data
19 litterbag measurements
Goodness of fit
R² = 0.84
Enhancement factor
m = 2.38 ± 0.56

Why the cuticle matters

The Pusa Decomposer underperforms because its microbes cannot get through the waxy cuticle that seals rice straw. DIVA's cold plasma breaks that outer layer first so the microbes reach the material underneath.

Corrections log

We fix our numbers in public.

A project that never corrects itself is not checking. These are the changes we made after going back to the sources.

Nitrogen lost to burning

Was

90% of the straw's nitrogen volatilised

Now

80%, matching the verified sources

Pusa Decomposer claim

Was

"+40% organic carbon" from the decomposer

Now

Dropped. We found no verified source for paddy.

DIVA electrode stack

Found

An air void inside the electrode sleeve was taking 45.3% of the applied voltage

Now

Identified and resolved in the electrode design

Government roles

Was

SDO Egra listed as issuing a mandate

Now

Mandates come from BDO Kolaghat and ADA Egra. The SDO gives support. Corrected September 2026.

DIVA architecture

Was

Rotating spoke wheel of plasma wafers

Now

Horizontal drum with fixed axial electrodes, so straw cannot wrap
