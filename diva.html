DIVA

Clean fields without a single match.

Dielectric Ionization Vortex Accelerator. A cold plasma drum that rides on the combine harvester and opens the waxy skin of rice straw, so it can rot in time for the next sowing.

Take the drum apart Ten days, not twenty-five

01 · Cold plasma

Lightning without the fire.

High voltage across glass barriers makes a dielectric barrier discharge. The electrons are energetic. The gas stays near room temperature. No flame, no smoke, nothing to catch.

02 · The unit cell

Mesh, glass, gap, ground.

The bench prototype stacks a copper mesh electrode over a borosilicate barrier, a discharge gap the straw passes through, and a ground electrode.

DriveFlexible SMPS, ZVS driver, flyback
HV bus≈40 kV
BarrierBorosilicate glass

03 · The drum

Ten electrodes, one turning drum.

Ten glass-sleeved electrodes run the full length of a steel chamber, 500 mm across. Each wears a 12 mm collar of glowing air, outside the glass. Straw tumbles along them, never across them, so nothing can wrap. Only the earthed shell turns.

04 · On the harvester

Same pass. No extra trip.

The chamber mounts under the harvester's exhaust, tilted 6°. Straw falls in the high end, tumbles for about two seconds and falls out the low end, treated. No extra pass, no extra fuel, no extra day.

The lock nobody picked

The real constraint isn't technology. It's time.

Time is set by one thing: the plant cuticle, the stratum cuticulare. Biology's waterproof armour, built to lock moisture in and keep microbes out. Nature takes 60 days to crack it. Pusa, the best decomposer on the market, needs 25. Farmers have 14.

Every other fix fought the symptom. DIVA picks the lock. Cold plasma strips hydrogen and splits carbon chains on the cuticle surface, the wax oxidises and cracks open, and moisture and microbes reach the cellulose underneath for the first time. The Pusa Decomposer then finishes the job in days instead of weeks.

The chamber · production unit

Every rotation turns the bed over.

A flat wafer treats one face of one stalk for under a millisecond. A bladed wheel puts metal across a 3.5 kg/s straw stream. The chamber gives every stalk a new face to the plasma on every turn. No stalk stays buried. None flies past untreated.

The physics

Hot electrons, cool gas.

A carbon-hydrogen bond takes about 4 eV to break. DIVA's electrons carry 4 to 5 eV on average. Each self-extinguishing microdischarge lives for nanoseconds and throws off reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, energetic electrons and UV. The straw stays cold throughout.

How it got here

Wafer, wheel, chamber.

Each version solved the last one's failure. The wafer proved the physics. The wheel would wrap. The chamber lets straw flow along the electrodes and tumble through the plasma.

Built and verified

The wafer

Woven copper mesh, borosilicate glass, a 9 to 10 mm plasma gap, copper foil ground and an acrylic base. The science, proven on a bench.

Dropped

Spoke wheel

Wafers arranged radially around a hub. A bladed wheel puts metal across the straw stream, and straw wraps anything it crosses.

Next build

Risk mapping

What could break, and what we did about it.

Eight engineering and adoption risks, placed by probability and severity. Pick a number to see the mitigation.

ProbabilitySeverity
CatastrophicCriticalMarginalNegligible FrequentHighHighSeriousMedium ProbableHighHighSeriousMedium OccasionalHighSeriousMediumLow RemoteSeriousMediumMediumLow ImprobableMediumMediumMediumLow
  1. Vibration and durability under continuous runningElectrodes are stationary and axial. Only the earthed shell rotates, on trunnion bearings. No rotating HV contacts anywhere.
  2. Mounting differs across harvester modelsTwo-SKU strategy: West Bengal track combines first, Punjab self-propelled machines on a separate bracket.
  3. Dust and moisture at the discharge zoneSealed housing. Self-extinguishing microdischarges mean no ignition risk.
  4. Power draw upsetting the harvester's electricsA buffer battery isolates the spiky HV load. The farmer's electronics never see the transient.
  5. Glass sleeve wear and dust trackingSleeves are passive consumables. Tool-free swap, replaced as a set each season.
  6. Regulatory clearance for HV on farm machineryEarly engagement through the existing BDO Kolaghat, ADA Egra and SDO relationships.
  7. CHC reluctance to modify owned equipmentBolt-on retrofit. Fully reversible, no permanent modification.
  8. Rotation speed mismatch at varying ground speedEliminated by design. A 60:1 worm gearbox off the chopper shaft keeps chamber speed and ground speed together.

Who pays

Priced for a Custom Hiring Centre.

We don't need farmers to pay for this. The Crop Residue Management scheme already subsidises anti-stubble-burning machinery at 80% for Custom Hiring Centres. At ₹35,000 a unit, government pays ₹28,000 and the CHC pays about ₹7,000.
